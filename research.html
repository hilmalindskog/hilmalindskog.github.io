Research

Dissertation

My dissertation investigates the influence of education on political attitudes. The project sits at the intersection of political sociology and political economy, and examines how schooling shapes the values, beliefs, and political behavior of citizens.

I approach this question from two angles: first, through processes occurring during the time spent in education — including peer socialization, exposure to educational content, and institutional effects; second, through processes in the transition to the labor market, where education shapes occupational outcomes and the social positions from which political orientations develop.

Research Interests

Education and Political Attitudes

Why does education correlate so strongly with political values and behavior? My work disentangles socialization effects from selection effects, drawing on longitudinal data and natural experiments to identify causal mechanisms. I am particularly interested in how education affects socio-cultural attitudes — such as authoritarian-libertarian values — and political participation.

Radical Right Voting and Social Context

A second strand of my research examines how voters act and express themselves politically based on their social environment. I study how the behaviors and attitudes of voters supporting radical right parties relate to the social acceptance of these parties in their local context — including how stigma shapes the visibility of radical right support in public and semi-public settings.

Methods

I work primarily with quantitative methods, including survey data analysis, longitudinal panel studies, and register-based data. I have experience with causal inference methods and large-scale administrative data.
